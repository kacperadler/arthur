# arthur continue

Usage: `arthur continue <id> <instruction> [--budget <usd> [--decision <id>]] [--ignore-restart]`

Resumes a job that has a branch of its own, a worker for example, and is `ended` or `blocked` at the end of its run (see `arthur jobs --help`), in place, with one more instruction as one quoted argument, and prints `<id>: continued`; the job is named by its full id or its short id, as `arthur jobs` shows it. It keeps the id, the record, the branch and the worktree, and the agent continues its last session there: the wrapper starts again (see `arthur spawn --help`) and runs `<agent> --continue ... -- <instruction>` in place of `@<record>/task`: after `--`, an instruction that starts with `-` or `@` is text, not an option of the agent or a file it includes. The instruction is in the record as `continue`, with a word joiner (U+2060) before each `@` omp would read a file for, as in `task` (see `arthur spawn --help`), and the log says `arthur continue: <first line>`. Before the wrapper starts, the job moves to `working` and its reason is cleared, emitting `job-state`. `arthur continue` returns once the wrapper wrote its `pid`; `job-ended` comes again at the new end. A wrapper that does not start ends the job `ended`, reason `failed: <reason>`, as at a spawn (see `arthur spawn --help`), and `arthur continue` prints `<id>: failed: <reason>` on stderr and exits 1.

- `--budget <usd>`: replaces the job's `budget`, a plain decimal of 0 or more (`0` is no limit). The record's `cost` counts on from the runs before, so a job that ended at its budget is stopped again at once without a higher one.
- `--ignore-restart`: a switch, no value, as at `arthur spawn --help`: the session plugin's restart guard lets this continue start while a restart is due; every other check still applies.
- The `timeout` stays the job's and counts from the new start.
- The caps are those in force now: the one plugin that provides `jobs.limits` is asked again, as at a spawn, and its answer replaces the record's `cpu`, `memory`, `feature`, `feature-cpu` and `feature-memory` before the wrapper starts, so a ticket whose `caps` the human raised (or lowered) has its slice, which all its jobs share, set to them, not back to those of the job's spawn (see "Caps per job" in `arthur spawn --help`). Two plugins that provide it refuse the continue, as a spawn.
- The role's MCP servers are those in force now: the `roles.model` answer for the kind's role is asked again, and its servers go into the record's `mcp/` before the wrapper starts, which the run's end removed (see `arthur spawn --help`); a role that has none now starts without them, and a job on another machine gets none.
- Once the checks pass, each plugin's `prepare` runs again on the job as its record has it, with the `--budget` given and the caps in force now, told it is a continue (see `arthur spawn --help`): the variables they return, such as the models plugin's provider for a job on a provider model, go into the new run's environment as at the spawn, never into the record. A step that sets up the worktree (the commit-rules hook, the projects install) does nothing, the worktree keeping what its spawn made.
- Before the wrapper starts, `job-started` is emitted again as at a spawn, with the record's `id`, `kind`, `project`, `root`, `branch`, `worktree` and `tickets`: a worker's tickets in `review` or `done` move back to `in-progress`, one already there is not written, and other kinds move none (see `arthur spawn --help`).
- `commits` at the end lists the branch's commits since the job's first `base`, every run's together; `result`, `tool-calls` and `activity` are the new run's.
- When a wrapper of the job still runs (the last one writing its end, or one a killed `arthur continue` started that has not written its `pid` yet; told by its command line, not by the record's `pid`), it waits up to 5 seconds for it to exit, since the new one starts in a scope of the same name and two never run for one job.
- A job on another machine (its record's `machine`; see `arthur spawn --help`) continues there: once the checks here pass (its state here, its branch, the limits, the `beforeSpawn` checks), the prepares run here, then `arthur remote-job continue` does there over SSH what is said above, the instruction and the prepares' variables on its stdin, in the scope of the jobs' slice there, whose limit is the machine's own (see `arthur remote-job --help`); then the job here moves to `working` and `job-started` is emitted here with its `machine`. The sweep copies its run from there as after a spawn. A wrapper that does not start there ends the job there, and its end is copied here at once.

Refused (exit code 1, the reason after `arthur continue: `):

- An id with no record in `<home>/jobs/`: `<id>: no such job in <home>/jobs`.
- A short id that matches two jobs: `<id> matches 2 jobs: <id>, <id>; name one by its full id`.
- A job in a run (`working`, `idle`, or held `blocked` by a plugin): `<id> is <state> (<reason>), in a run; a job in a run is not continued, arthur steer gives it a correction`.
- Another `arthur continue` of the same job, from before it reads the job's state until its new wrapper has started or the continue fails: `<id>: being continued`. That continue holds the file `<home>/jobs/<id>/continuing`, which names its pid (and, on Linux, its boot) meanwhile; one left by a continue that was killed, whose pid is dead or from an earlier boot, is taken over by the next continue. For a job on another machine the file here is released as soon as `arthur remote-job continue` returns, since the wrapper there removes only the lock there.
- A wrapper of the job still running 5 seconds after every other check passed: `<id>: its wrapper <pid> still runs 5 seconds on; a second one is not started beside it`. Nothing in the record is written; once it has ended, the continue goes through.
- A read-only kind, which has no branch of its own: `<id> is a <kind>, a read-only kind without a branch of its own; only a job with a branch is continued`.
- A job whose worktree is gone, as `arthur prune` leaves one whose branch has commits Git refuses to delete: `<id>: its worktree was pruned; spawn a new job on its branch <branch>`. The branch keeps the work: a new worker starts from HEAD on a branch of its own, so its task names `<branch>` to merge or build on. Nothing in the record is written.
- An empty argument, the id or the instruction (`""`): wrong usage, the usage line and exit code 2, before anything is run. A whitespace-only instruction: `the instruction is empty`.
- On another machine, a refusal from there, each as above (in a run there, its worktree pruned there, being continued there), after `machine <name>: arthur remote-job continue: `. A machine that does not answer: `machine <name>: ssh <ssh>: <ssh's last line>`. A machine no plugin registers now: `<id> runs on machine <name>, which no plugin registers; machines: <names>`.
- A plugin's `beforeSpawn` check, run on the job as its record has it (its `task` and the `Ticket:` files it names, `kind`, `project`, `model`, `thinking`, `timeout`, and the `--budget` or else its `budget`), that gives a reason: `refused by <plugin>: <reason>`; one that throws: `plugin <plugin>: beforeSpawn failed: <message>`. The core's own check refuses a worker whose ticket cannot move to in-progress, a `merged` one for example, as at a spawn (see `arthur spawn --help`). A refused continue starts nothing and leaves the record as it was.
- A plugin's `prepare` that throws, or returns anything but variables: `plugin <plugin>: prepare failed: <message>`, as at a spawn; the continue starts nothing and leaves the record as it was.
- A config or a plugin that does not load: `arthur.config.ts did not load:`, then the reasons.

Wrong usage (not one id and one instruction, an unknown flag, `--budget` without a plain decimal or given twice) prints the usage line and exits 2.

`--budget 0` on a job whose record is not `free` hands the checks `--decision <id>` (`""` without it) with the job's `id`, as `arthur spawn --help` says under "--budget 0": the owner's `budget` decision for the job's ticket, or for this job (`--job <id>`) when it names none, used once. A plugin's `host.jobs.continue(id, instruction, budgetUsd, decision)` hands its `decision` the same way; without one it is not checked. A continue without `--budget` keeps the job's budget.
